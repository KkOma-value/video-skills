# Reference Style Bible: Faceted Noir Lyric Film

Use this as an abstract visual and editorial study. Recreate its structural effect with new user-supplied characters, lyrics, symbols, and title; do not copy literal source subjects, onscreen words, or frames.

## Measured format and pacing

- Duration: 30.12 seconds; 16:9 horizontal; 30 fps; supplied file is 1882×1080.
- Structure: nine major visual groups, with only a few true cuts. The strongest transitions arrive around 2.1, 5.8, 8.3, 10.1, 16.2, 19.3, 22.1, 23.8, and 26.2 seconds (tolerate roughly ±0.2 s when adapting).
- Mix behavior: continuous music/vocal energy with no detected pauses longer than 0.2 s at −35 dB. Measured integrated loudness is about −11.9 LUFS with a 5.6 LU range—dense and intentional rather than ambient.
- Subtitle behavior: lyric phrases, usually a compact Chinese line and a smaller English line, appear at lower center. They reinforce rhythm without becoming the visual subject.

## Visual grammar

### Palette

Use the following as approximate roles, not mandatory sampled values:

| Role | Approximate color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Void / outlines | `#070B0D` | Background, hair, silhouette, negative space. |
| Slate facets | `#252A2D` | Architecture and low-contrast shape separation. |
| Bone / light | `#E0D6C4` | Face planes, wings, title/emblem, rare relief. |
| Oxblood | `#760F15` | Ground, drama fields, large tension wedges. |
| Signal red | `#D0342C` | Flowers, micro-accents, a few directional planes. |
| Deep teal | `#123B39` | Supporting planes that cool red/cream contrast. |
| Jade light | `#29B79F` | Symbol, magical energy, fissure only. |
| Pale mint | `#9BDFCB` | Brief bloom around jade, never a background wash. |

### Shapes and material

- Build surfaces from hard triangular/quadrilateral planes with two or three values per object. Let an object look cut, folded, or illustrated—not extruded.
- Combine faceted forms with sparse hairline geometry, thread-like contours, and a little printed-paper or ink grain.
- Make silhouettes legible first. Make details serve the silhouette rather than decorating every empty plane.
- Use circles/ovals for ritual attention (eye, cup, portal, halo) and narrow diagonal wedges for attack, flight, or fate.
- Treat the frame like an editorial poster: a few deliberately placed masses beat many tiny objects.

### Light and camera

- Let light arrive as angular slabs, not soft studio gradients. Black must remain deep enough for cream and jade to cut through.
- Work in 2.5D layers. The reference's sense of movement mainly comes from slow camera pushes, lateral drifts, and shape changes—not from complex character acting.
- Save high velocity for the bloom, launch, crack, or burst. The contrast between stillness and one event creates impact.

### Typography

- Set lyric subtitles as a rhythm device: compact, lower-center, off-white, and subordinate to the image.
- Keep each phrase to one or two readable lines. Leave roughly 8% of frame height below it.
- Use a narrow, high-contrast serif or equivalent distinctive type for the final title. Do not imitate the reference wording or logo.

## Why the reference feels unified

1. **A recurring motif carries causality.** A small jade object moves from emotion to ritual, flight, rupture, and release.
2. **Color has grammar.** Red means tension/ground, cream means revelation, jade means the supernatural or decisive change, black holds the world together.
3. **The shot order alternates intimacy and scale.** Portrait → symbolic insert → tableau → large metaphor → labyrinth creates breadth without requiring literal plot.
4. **Motion is rationed.** Most frames behave like moving posters; one controlled change per beat makes the edit feel intentional.
5. **The finish earns its stillness.** The title does not arrive at the peak; it follows the release and is allowed to breathe.

## Failure patterns to avoid

- Treating low-poly as a generic 3D render rather than flat illustrated facets.
- Filling every frame with red, glow, particles, or motion.
- Switching character design, facet scale, or palette family between clips.
- Asking generated video to invent six seconds of complex acting or transformation in one pass.
- Using the literal source title, subject, or recognizable signature object instead of the user's material.
